# ArbiterSTG 2.0: Structural Trace Governance

## Abstract

ArbiterSTG is a post-execution structural trace-governance framework. It classifies what happens to residue after execution has occurred. It is concerned with trace admission, limitation, masking, routing, shadowing, transfer, and nulling.

ArbiterSTG does not authorize execution. It does not decide truth, meaning, origin, ownership, value, legal liability, moral guilt, safety, or final accountability. Its purpose is narrower: to prevent post-execution trace handling from being mistaken for proof, origin, observer completion, or institutional closure.

## 1. Core Principle

Execution precedes interpretation.

Trace persists unevenly.

Observer access is not guaranteed.

Post-execution routing must not be mistaken for truth, origin, or governance.

## 2. Parent Architecture

ArbiterSTG operates downstream of Machine-Dream Syntax (MDS).

```text
Machine-Dream Syntax (MDS) -> ArbiterSTG
```

MDS supplies the execution-observer-residue structure. ArbiterSTG supplies post-execution trace classification and routing logic.

MDS governing equation:

```text
S = (F -> O)[Delta T] + D/L +/- R
```

The equation names a non-coincidence between execution and observer stabilization. ArbiterSTG does not collapse that interval into a claim of completion. It treats residue as structurally uneven, potentially degraded, inaccessible, assigned, transferred, or lost.

## 3. Scope

ArbiterSTG asks:

- What residue exists?
- What residue is accessible?
- What residue is admitted?
- What residue is routed?
- What residue is masked?
- What residue is shadowed?
- What residue is transferred?
- What residue is nulled?
- What claims exceed the trace?

These questions do not produce final judgment. They produce trace classification.

## 4. Residue Classes

| Class | Name | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `R_p` | Produced residue | Residue generated by or after execution. |
| `R_a` | Accessible residue | Produced residue available for structured review. |
| `R_s` | Assigned residue | Residue assigned to an interpretive, institutional, proxy, or procedural route. |
| `R_u` | Unreachable residue | Produced residue that persists but is not directly accessible. |
| `R_b` | Bridge residue | Residue that supports movement from unreachable to accessible status. |

## 5. Trace Modes

| Mode | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Admission | Admits trace into the post-execution record without validating meaning, truth, guilt, validity, or ownership. |
| Limited Admission | Admits trace under constraint and marks incompleteness, bridge dependence, proxy limits, or unresolved routing. |
| Masking | Identifies hidden, flattened, displaced, absorbed, or misleadingly categorized trace. |
| Routing | Directs trace through an interpretive, institutional, procedural, technical, legal, narrative, or proxy pathway. |
| Shadow | Marks trace that persists but remains unresolved, inaccessible, ambiguous, underdetermined, or structurally unavailable for full admission. |
| Transfer | Marks trace moved from one route, actor, system, proxy, institution, context, or interpretive layer to another. |
| Null | Marks trace that cannot be recruited into the record or is treated as non-actionable, absent, destroyed, inaccessible, or unrecoverable. |

## 6. Limited Admission

Limited Admission is a constrained admission state. It allows trace into the record while preventing overclaiming.

It is appropriate when:

- residue exists but remains incomplete;
- access depends on bridge residue;
- support is proxy-mediated;
- routing remains unresolved;
- source material is degraded;
- observer access is partial;
- the available trace cannot carry the claim being made.

Limited Admission protects the record from two opposite errors: erasing residue because it is incomplete, and overvalidating residue because it is present.

## 7. Lacunal Trace

Lacunal Trace is a trace condition where residue persists, but bridge formation fails or remains incomplete. It is not absence. It is a gap in accessibility, recruitability, or routing.

Lacunal Trace may result in Shadow, Transfer, Null, or Limited Admission. It should not be converted into full observer completion because an institution, system, or narrative pathway needs closure.

## 8. Collapse by Clarification

Collapse by Clarification occurs when attempts to clarify trace damage, distort, reduce, expose, or collapse the structure that allowed it to persist.

Some trace can be destroyed by the demand that it become fully explicit. ArbiterSTG therefore treats clarification as an operation with trace risk, not as a neutral good.

## 9. Authority Laundering

Authority Laundering occurs when institutional, procedural, legal, technical, or proxy authority misclassifies trace in order to make an unsupported claim appear legitimate.

Typical forms include:

- converting assignment into origin;
- treating routing as truth;
- treating proxy legibility as full residue recovery;
- presenting Limited Admission as full Admission;
- treating trace absence as non-execution;
- treating trace presence as meaning.

## 10. Indices

ArbiterSTG 2.0 uses two active indices:

- `RLCI`: Residue-Loss Criticality Index
- `RAI`: Residue Accessibility Index

Possible future indices include:

- `SPI`: Shadow Persistence Index
- `JTI`: Jurisdictional Trace Index

Future indices are not finalized unless explicitly adopted.

## 11. Boundary Statement

ArbiterSTG is non-governing. It is a diagnostic routing and classification layer.

Its outputs may support review, documentation, audit, or structured discussion, but they do not decide truth, legality, morality, safety, origin, ownership, or final accountability.
