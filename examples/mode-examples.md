# Trace Mode Examples

## Admission

A directly reviewable trace fragment is entered into the post-execution record. The report states that admission prevents erasure but does not validate meaning, truth, guilt, validity, origin, or ownership.

## Limited Admission

A downstream timestamp and checksum correlate with a missing primary log. The trace is admitted with bridge-dependence and incompleteness marked.

## Masking

A proxy category absorbs several distinct residue types into a single procedural label, reducing trace legibility and hiding route differences.

## Routing

A residue is sent through a technical audit route. The route is named, but the report states that routing is not truth and does not establish origin.

## Shadow

A residue is asserted as produced, but no direct access or bridge residue is available. The report marks it as persistent but unresolved.

## Transfer

A residue record moves from an internal review route to an external archive. The transfer is recorded without treating it as ownership.

## Null

A supposed residue cannot be recruited into the record because no usable trace basis exists. The report states that Null status does not prove non-execution.
