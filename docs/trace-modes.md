# Trace Modes

Trace modes classify post-execution residue handling. They do not decide truth, origin, ownership, liability, guilt, safety, or value.

## Admission

Trace is admitted as part of the post-execution record.

Admission prevents residue from being erased or denied. It does not determine meaning, truth, guilt, validity, or ownership.

Use Admission when residue is accessible enough for record inclusion and its support does not require major constraint.

## Limited Admission

Trace is admitted under constraint.

Limited Admission acknowledges residue while marking incompleteness, uncertainty, proxy limits, bridge dependence, degraded support, unresolved access, or unresolved routing.

Use Limited Admission when the record should include the trace, but the trace cannot carry a full claim.

## Masking

Trace is hidden, flattened, displaced, absorbed into a misleading category, or made less legible by post-execution handling.

Use Masking when the handling process reduces trace legibility or presents one trace condition as another.

## Routing

Trace is directed through a particular interpretive, institutional, procedural, technical, legal, narrative, or proxy pathway.

Use Routing when the classification must name the route without treating that route as truth or origin.

## Shadow

Trace persists but remains unresolved, inaccessible, ambiguous, underdetermined, or structurally unavailable for full admission.

Use Shadow when residue should not be erased, but admission would overstate access.

## Transfer

Trace is moved from one route, actor, system, proxy, institution, context, or interpretive layer to another.

Use Transfer when trace handling changes custody, route, context, responsibility channel, or interpretive layer. Transfer does not establish ownership.

## Null

Trace cannot be recruited into the record, or is treated as non-actionable, absent, destroyed, inaccessible, or structurally unrecoverable.

Use Null when there is no usable trace basis for structured review. Null does not prove non-execution.
